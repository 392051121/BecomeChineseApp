# BecomeChineseApp Product Design Document
目标人群：面向欧美市场喜欢中国文化的人，app以英文为主，必要时候中英对照。
不可突破底线：弘扬中国文化，不能出现日韩等泛东亚内容，不能抹黑污蔑中国
## 1. Product Vision

BecomeChineseApp is a cultural atlas for English-speaking users who want to understand Chinese history, daily life, regional food, and place-based identity.

The product is not a reference encyclopedia and not a decorative Chinese-style app. It is a modern cultural discovery app that helps users:

- understand how Chinese culture is organized through history, geography, food, and seasonal life
- see how ordinary life in China differs by region, city, and era
- build a personal atlas through collection, ritual completion, and exploration
- move from curiosity to comprehension through a simple and rewarding interaction loop

The app should feel:

- clear
- modern
- readable
- culturally distinctive
- welcoming to non-Chinese users
- structured like a content product, not a museum display

---

## 2. Audience

### Primary audience
English-speaking users interested in Chinese culture, history, and everyday life.

### Secondary audience
- learners of Chinese language and culture
- travelers preparing for China
- diaspora users revisiting regional identity
- general cultural explorers who want a better entry point into Chinese life

### Audience needs
These users need:
- fast comprehension
- short explanations
- clear navigation
- visible progress
- cultural context without academic overload
- a way to connect what they read to what they save and complete

---

## 3. Product Positioning

The app should sit between a content feed, a cultural guide, and a personal atlas.

### It should not feel like:
- a textbook
- a static museum exhibit
- a generic encyclopedia
- a heavy traditional-art app

### It should feel like:
- a modern editorial culture app
- a curated discovery platform
- a personal learning atlas
- a daily ritual app with cultural depth

---

## 4. Core Experience Goal

The main experience should answer four questions quickly:

1. What is this part of Chinese culture?
2. Why should I care?
3. What should I do next?
4. What changed after I did it?

If the app answers those clearly, the product will feel strong to non-Chinese users.

---

## 5. Design Principles

### 5.1 Clarity over decoration
The app should use cultural styling carefully. Visual identity should support comprehension, not distract from it.

### 5.2 Content first
The user should always see the content before the system.

### 5.3 One primary action per screen
Every screen should make one main next step obvious.

### 5.4 Cultural meaning through structure
Chinese identity should come through language, hierarchy, geography, food, seasonality, and history relationships rather than surface ornament.

### 5.5 English-first readability
English is the primary reading layer. Chinese appears as a supporting layer, not as a barrier.

### 5.6 Progress must feel real
Actions should create visible, meaningful change in the user’s atlas.

---

## 6. Content Model

The app content should be organized around five cultural lenses:

- history
- food
- places
- seasons
- identity

### 6.1 History
Users learn how dynasties shaped culture, daily life, and worldview.

### 6.2 Food
Users understand dishes as regional stories, not just recipes.

### 6.3 Places
Users see cities as lived environments with pace, mood, and local character.

### 6.4 Seasons
Users complete a daily ritual that connects the calendar to cultural rhythm.

### 6.5 Identity
Users see their collected cultural path accumulate into a personal atlas.

---

## 7. Information Architecture

### 7.1 Primary navigation
- Home
- Seasons
- History
- Food
- Places
- Profile

### 7.2 Page roles

#### Home
Decision layer and starting point.

#### Seasons
Daily ritual entry and task completion.

#### History
Civilizational storytelling and historical exploration.

#### Food
Regional cuisine storytelling and cultural tasting.

#### Places
City reading, daily life, and regional character.

#### Profile
Identity archive, progress record, and atlas summary.

---

## 8. Screen-Level Design Direction

### 8.1 Home
Home should feel like a concise editorial dashboard.

#### What it should show
- today’s ritual
- recent progress summary
- current path snapshot
- a quick daily quiz entry
- clear module shortcuts

#### What it should not do
- overwhelm the user with equal-weight modules
- feel like a full destination page
- bury the primary action

#### Tone
Clear, concise, inviting, and decisive.

---

### 8.2 Seasons
Seasons should feel like a daily action page.

#### What it should show
- current date and seasonal context
- one daily prompt or quiz
- visible answer states
- streak and completion feedback

#### What it should not do
- become a long content page
- expose too many secondary modules
- dilute the sense of a daily task

#### Tone
Focused, ritual-like, and rewarding.

---

### 8.3 History
History should feel like an editorial timeline.

#### What it should show
- dynasty cards
- world context
- cultural contribution
- related paths to places and food
- expandable detail sections

#### What it should not do
- feel like a dry chronology table
- overload the user with too many emperor facts at once
- bury the next step

#### Tone
Curated, readable, and cinematic without being ornate.

---

### 8.4 Food
Food should feel like a regional story stream.

#### What it should show
- dish imagery
- province association
- cultural story
- taste profile
- substitution or home-version note
- etiquette or social context
- related place and history paths

#### What it should not do
- become a recipe database
- read like a food glossary
- treat cuisine as isolated from place and history

#### Tone
Warm, appetizing, and interpretive.

---

### 8.5 Places
Places should feel like city essays.

#### What it should show
- city image
- region and province context
- character or vibe
- local life notes
- insider tips
- highlights
- related food and history paths

#### What it should not do
- let the geographic entry dominate the whole screen
- make the user feel like they are in a map tool
- hide the content flow below a large map block

#### Tone
Observational, friendly, and easy to browse.

---

### 8.6 Profile
Profile should feel like an identity altar and archive.

#### What it should show
- generated or saved name identity
- atlas map of connected provinces
- badges and rank
- progress stats
- saved items
- shareable identity summary

#### What it should not do
- feel like a settings page
- fragment the user’s identity into unrelated modules
- over-explain what the page is

#### Tone
Reflective, proud, and complete.

---

## 9. Visual Strategy

### 9.1 Overall visual direction
Move from a heavy paper-scroll aesthetic to a lighter modern editorial style with subtle cultural cues.

### 9.2 Visual characteristics
- warm light background
- strong but restrained Chinese red
- clear cards and boundaries
- generous spacing
- minimal texture
- crisp type hierarchy
- subtle stamp or seal accents

### 9.3 What to avoid
- overly heavy parchment effects
- too much antique texture
- dark, dense visual framing
- decorative overload
- page layouts that feel like exhibition boards

### 9.4 Chinese cultural cues to emphasize
- red accents
- seal/stamp feedback
- province and city relationships
- dynasty and season naming
- bilingual hierarchy
- map-based identity growth

---

## 10. Interaction Strategy

### 10.1 Core interaction loop
Discover → Tap → Feedback → Progress → Archive → Continue

### 10.2 Interaction goals
The UI should make the user feel:
- they know what to do
- they know what happened
- they can keep going
- they have made progress

### 10.3 Interaction language
Use a consistent vocabulary for:
- completion
- saved state
- unlocked state
- related path continuation
- streak growth
- identity growth

### 10.4 Feedback types
- stamp animation
- haptic pulse
- highlighted selected state
- saved state badge
- solved state
- unlocked province state
- shareable identity card

---

## 11. Copy Strategy

### 11.1 English-first
English should lead the copy system.

### 11.2 Short and informative
Avoid long explanatory blocks unless they add real understanding.

### 11.3 Cultural translation, not literal translation
Chinese labels should support the English meaning, not compete with it.

### 11.4 Style examples
Preferred:
- `Open today’s one task`
- `Continue the thread`
- `Related Paths`
- `Read Aloud`
- `Save`

Avoid:
- overly academic phrasing
- empty poetic lines that do not help comprehension
- too many metaphor-only labels

---

## 12. Relationship Model

The app should connect content through province-based relationships.

### Relationship types
- city ↔ food
- city ↔ dynasty
- food ↔ dynasty
- province ↔ identity
- ritual ↔ progress

### Why this matters
Users should be able to move through the app by following real cultural connections rather than isolated pages.

---

## 13. Success Criteria

The UI and content design are successful if a user can:

- open the app and immediately know what to do
- understand what each module is for
- browse content without feeling lost
- complete one daily action easily
- feel rewarded by the response
- see their progress accumulate in Profile
- move through Chinese culture in a way that feels natural and understandable

---

## 14. Design Summary

The app should feel like:
- a modern editorial culture product
- a clear learning atlas
- a personal progress archive
- a culturally distinctive but readable app for non-Chinese users

The visual system should support the story, not dominate it.
The content should create the cultural depth.
The interactions should create the sense of progress.
The Profile page should make all of it feel connected.
